Case Study: Gender Bias in Facial Recognition Systems in India

Case:
Research on commercial facial recognition systems has identified
gender-related differences in classification errors, raising concerns
about deployment in policing and public services.

Root causes:
Potential contributors include inadequate demographic representation
in training and evaluation datasets, image-quality differences,
algorithm design limitations, and insufficient testing across diverse
Indian populations. Locally sourced data alone does not guarantee
fairness.

Proposed fixes:
Developers should conduct independent and recurring audits across
relevant demographic groups, improve dataset diversity and quality,
publish subgroup-specific performance metrics, and test systems under
realistic deployment conditions. High-impact decisions should include
human review, accountability, and an accessible appeal process.

Conclusion:
AI systems must be evaluated for unequal error rates as well as
overall accuracy before deployment, especially where errors may
affect people's rights and safety.

Reference:
Basheer, I. P. (2025). Bias in the Algorithm: Issues Raised Due to
Use of Facial Recognition in India. Journal of Development Policy
and Practice, 10(1), 61–79.
